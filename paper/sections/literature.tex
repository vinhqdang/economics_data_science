\section{Related literature and the research gap}\label{sec:lit}

\paragraph{Model averaging and forecast combination.}
Since \citet{BatesGranger1969}, combining forecasts has been one of the most reliable ways to improve predictive accuracy; \citet{Timmermann2006} and \citet{Wang2023review} review five decades of work.
Frequentist model averaging chooses weights by minimising an estimate of risk---a Mallows criterion \citep{Hansen2007}, leave-one-out or $K$-fold cross-validation \citep{HansenRacine2012,ZhangLiu2023}, or forward validation for time series \citep{ZhangZhang2023}---and establishes asymptotic optimality, i.e.\ that the chosen weights attain the lowest attainable risk.
The framework now covers dependent data \citep{ZhangWanZou2013}, quantile losses \citep{LuSu2015}, general losses \citep{Yu2024unified}, time-varying weights \citep{Sun2021tvma}, covariate-dependent weights \citep{Gong2026moe}, covariate shift \citep{Zhang2026shift} and prediction intervals \citep{Qu2025intervals}.
The forecast-combination puzzle---that equal weights frequently beat estimated weights---is attributed mainly to estimation error in the weights \citep{SmithWallis2009,Claeskens2016}; remedies shrink estimated weights towards equal weights \citep{DieboldShin2019} or restrict them \citep{Zou2025weights}.
When many series must be forecast, weights are either estimated series by series or learned from series features \citep[FFORMA,][]{MonteroManso2020}; \citet{AiolfiTimmermann2006} cluster \emph{forecasting models} by past performance.
All of these approaches either treat each target series separately or impose one rule on all of them, and none groups the \emph{target units}.

\paragraph{Decision-focused learning.}
Operations research has long recognised that the loss that matters is the decision cost \citep{Granger1969}.
The data-driven newsvendor literature learns order quantities directly from data \citep{Levi2015,BanRudin2019}; prescriptive analytics maps covariates to decisions \citep{BertsimasKallus2020}; and ``smart predict-then-optimise'' trains predictors on decision regret \citep{ElmachtoubGrigas2022}; see \citet{Mandi2024} for a survey.
In energy forecasting, \citet{Wang2019cqra} combine probabilistic load forecasts by minimising pinball loss on the simplex, one series at a time.
This literature trains single models or combines forecasts for one unit; it does not exploit cross-sectional structure when many heterogeneous units face related decisions.

\paragraph{Deep learning for forecasting and for combination weights.}
Pretrained time-series foundation models such as Chronos \citep{Ansari2024chronos} now forecast unseen series zero-shot with accuracy close to models trained on the target data, and deep networks are increasingly used to \emph{produce} combination weights: FFORMA maps series features to weights with gradient boosting \citep{MonteroManso2020}, and mixture-of-experts model averaging uses a softmax neural gate with covariate-dependent weights \citep{Gong2026moe}.
These approaches are flexible, but they fit thousands of parameters, their weights are hard to audit, and---except for \citet{Gong2026moe}, who study conditional generative models---they come without optimality guarantees for the combined decision.
We include both a foundation model (as a candidate) and a decision-focused neural gate (as a competitor) in our application.

\paragraph{Storage siting.}
Where to place grid-scale batteries is usually decided with engineering models of the transmission network, solved by decomposition or stochastic programming \citep{Piansky2024battery,Larroux2026bess}.
These models take the uncertainty that storage must absorb as an input.
We make that input endogenous: the residual commitment risk depends on how operators combine their forecasts, so the value and the best location of storage depend on the forecasting policy.

\paragraph{Latent group structures.}
Panel econometrics models unobserved heterogeneity through latent groups estimated by $k$-means-type algorithms \citep{LinNg2012,BonhommeManresa2015,AndoBai2016} or classifier-Lasso \citep{Su2016}.
These methods group \emph{regression parameters} under squared loss and focus on the parameters themselves, not on combining the outputs of existing forecasting systems under an asymmetric decision loss.

\paragraph{The gap.}
Table~\ref{tab:gap} summarises the position of GDMA.
No existing method simultaneously (a) combines a library of existing candidate rules, including machine-learning and foundation-model forecasts, (b) chooses weights by the downstream decision cost with unit-specific cost asymmetries, and (c) shares statistical strength across many decision units through latent segments whose number is chosen from data, with guarantees on optimality and segment recovery; nor does any link the resulting operational risk to the siting of new storage.
Filling this gap matters most when windows are short---because systems change and old data are stale---and units are many, which is exactly the situation of system-wide infrastructure planning.
