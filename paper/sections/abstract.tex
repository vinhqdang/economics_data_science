\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Grid operators must commit capacity a day ahead, and planners must decide where to install new batteries.
Both decisions rest on how the many available demand forecasts---operators' own forecasts, statistical models, machine learning and pretrained foundation models---are combined.
We propose \emph{grouped decision-focused model averaging} (GDMA), which jointly learns a small number of latent segments of decision units and segment-specific combination weights by minimising the realised decision cost, with each unit's own cost asymmetry, rather than forecast error; a soft version shrinks each unit's weights towards its segment and nests pooled weights, unit-specific weights and shrinkage.
We prove a finite-sample oracle inequality that separates the cost of learning segment memberships from the cost of learning weights, show that GDMA is asymptotically optimal and attains the risk of the infeasible unit-specific optimum when segments exist, and prove that it recovers the segments with probability approaching one.
A planning stage turns the residual risk of the learned rule into an optimal siting of storage.
On 125 grid areas in North America, Europe, Oceania, Asia and Africa with an average demand of 1.66\,TW, using public hourly data from 2018 to 2026, soft GDMA lowers out-of-sample commitment cost by 17\% worldwide (18--20\% for the U.S.\ operators, relative to their official forecasts), outperforms combining the same forecasts for accuracy, and outperforms a decision-focused neural mixture-of-experts gate by nine percentage points.
Better combination substitutes for storage: without batteries, soft GDMA is cheaper than the status quo with 60\,GW of optimally sited batteries, and it is worth 6--8\,GW relative to standard combination rules.
Where a storage programme should go depends on the commitment rule; siting on the status-quo error profile loses up to 15\% of a small programme's value.
\par\medskip\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} model averaging; decision-focused learning; latent groups; foundation models; capacity commitment; battery storage siting; energy management
\end{abstract}
